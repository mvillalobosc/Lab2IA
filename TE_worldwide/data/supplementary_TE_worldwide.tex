\subsection*{Data appendix (Table S3)}

Table S3 documents every variable with its indicator code, definition, unit, source, transformation and missing-data rule. The rules below apply to all variables.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Reference period.} Calendar years 2015 to 2020. Every value is the observation published for that calendar year; no value was carried forward or backward from another year, and no interpolation or imputation was applied in the main dataset (the single interpolated cell of the robustness extraction is documented in item 7).
\item \textbf{Complete-case rule for the DEA frontiers.} A country enters the frontier of a given year only if all three inputs and both outputs are observed for that year. This produced 83, 84, 84, 85, 71 and 59 countries in 2015 to 2020 (Table S2). Missing values were concentrated in physicians and hospital beds.
\item \textbf{Balanced panel and fixed sets.} The Malmquist analysis used the balanced panel of 34 countries defined in the source study as the countries present in every annual dataset at the time of that analysis.\citep{valenzuela2024} In the final annual frontiers reported in Table S6, 39 countries have a score in every year; this larger set is used for the descriptive re-tabulation (Table S8) and for the fixed-sample frontiers (Table S11), which do not depend on the panel structure of the Malmquist index.
\item \textbf{Second-stage models.} Country-year observations enter the second-stage models when the efficiency score and all contextual variables are observed: 445 of the 466 country-years (the 21 exclusions lack public education expenditure). The year-specific models use 76, 76, 83, 82, 70 and 58 observations for 2015 to 2020.
\item \textbf{Group classifications.} Geographic regions (continents) and subregions follow the World Bank classification. Income groups follow the World Bank income classification recorded in the source dataset for each country (Table S6). The financing model (public, mixed, private) follows the Commonwealth Fund's international health care system profiles, which classify systems by their main source of financing.\citep{commonwealth2024} The income-group frontiers of Table S13 merge the low- and lower-middle-income groups because the low-income group contains three to seven countries per year.
\item \textbf{Reporting quality.} Physician and bed densities are compiled by WHO from national administrative sources and differ in periodicity and completeness; life expectancy and infant mortality are estimates produced by the UN Population Division and the UN Inter-agency Group for Child Mortality Estimation and are available for all countries and years. The countries that lack a score in 2019 and 2020 are therefore mainly those with irregular workforce or bed reporting, and Table S8 documents their characteristics.
\item \textbf{Software.} The main analysis (annual DEA frontiers, specification comparison, Malmquist index, Kruskal--Wallis tests and the original Tobit models) was run in R 4.3.1 with the rDEA package.\citep{valenzuela2024} The robustness analyses and the second-stage models reported in this article (Tables S9 to S15 and Tables 2 and 3 of the main text) were computed in Python 3.11 with SciPy 1.17 (radial DEA solved as linear programmes with the HiGHS solver; maximum-likelihood Tobit and truncated regressions; bootstrap with 500 replications for the Tobit models, 2,000 for Algorithm 1 and 100/1,000 for Algorithm 2 of Simar and Wilson). The code is available from the corresponding author.
\item \textbf{Two data extractions.} The main analysis uses the extraction made for the source study in 2024. The robustness analyses use a new extraction from the World Bank API made on 27 September 2026, restricted to the same 466 country-year cells; one cell (Albania 2018, physicians) is no longer published and was interpolated between 2016 and 2019. Re-estimating the annual IO--CRS frontiers on the new extraction reproduces the published scores closely (Spearman rank correlation 0.94 to 0.99 by year, 0.98 pooled; mean absolute difference 0.03; the same eight to nine frontier countries in most years). The largest differences involve countries whose physician or bed series were revised by WHO after 2024 (Liberia, Togo, Bhutan, Thailand, Kazakhstan).
\end{enumerate}
